# Competitor desk audit

**Method:** Review the publicly available official product/help pages listed below. This is a high-level feature and positioning audit, not a usability benchmark. Access date: 27 October 2026.

| Product | Public-page observation | UX question / design opportunity | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pucho.ai | Public materials describe AI agents, visual workflows, connected tools, and human-in-the-loop modes. | How can a first-time user inspect the plan, data access, and consequential actions before a workflow runs? | https://www.pucho.ai/documentation/product-overview/pucho-platform |
| Zapier | Help documentation describes a Zap as a trigger plus one or more actions connected across apps. | Can the user clearly predict what starts the automation and what changes as a result? | https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8496309697421-What-is-a-Zap |
| Make | Its product page emphasises a visual-first automation platform, AI assistance and visible orchestration/data flows. | Can the visual sequence be understandable without requiring technical vocabulary? | https://www.make.com/en/product |
| n8n | Its AI-agent page describes inspecting inputs and outputs, replaying runs, and setting human checkpoints for actions. | Can audit, failure explanation and recovery be available in simple language? | https://n8n.io/ai-agents/ |

## Synthesis

The case-study opportunity is not to claim one competitor is objectively better. It is to combine a clear, outcome-first entry point with a readable plan, limited and explicit permissions, a preview, an approval checkpoint for consequential actions, and understandable run history/recovery.

## Limits

- Public positioning is evidence of advertised capabilities, not evidence of usability or user satisfaction.
- No controlled side-by-side task evaluation was performed.
- Product features and claims can change; re-check the official pages before external publication.
