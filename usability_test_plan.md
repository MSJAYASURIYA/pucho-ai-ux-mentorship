# Usability validation plan

## Research question
Can a non-technical user understand, inspect and control a proposed AI automation before it runs, and recover confidently when a step fails?

## Participants
Aim for 5–8 people who regularly do administrative or operations work. If that is not feasible, start with 3 people and note the limitation. Avoid collecting real customer or confidential business data.

## Session format
20–30 minutes per participant. Ask for think-aloud feedback. Do not teach the interface while the task is in progress. Ask a short follow-up only after the task attempt.

## Tasks

1. **Create a workflow:** “Every morning, summarize new leads from a spreadsheet, highlight the most promising ones, and prepare a team digest.”
   - Observe whether the participant can begin with natural language without needing to know technical terms.
2. **Review the AI plan and permissions:** Ask the participant to explain which data is used, which apps are involved, and whether a message will be sent automatically.
   - Observe misunderstanding, overlooked controls, uncertainty and incorrect assumptions.
3. **Recover from a problem:** Explain that a connected tool failed and ask the participant to find the safest next action.
   - Observe whether the participant can identify the failure, understand its impact, and find retry/edit/reconnect options.

## Capture for each task

- Task completion (success / partial / not completed)
- Time or major pauses (roughly; don't overfocus on speed)
- Misunderstandings and misclicks
- Participant's explanation of what the system will do
- Whether the participant notices permissions and approval
- Direct quotes, recorded accurately and with consent
- Severity and design implication

## What to change after testing

Prioritize issues that lead to an incorrect prediction of the workflow, accidental disclosure or sending, inability to locate the approval state, or inability to recover. Revise the design and re-test those tasks.
