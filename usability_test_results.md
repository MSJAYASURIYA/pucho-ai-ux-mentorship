## Research question

Can a first-time, non-technical user understand a proposed automation, review its access, inspect a preview, and identify a safe next step when something is unclear?

## Test setup

- **Target participants:** People who regularly handle repetitive administrative or operations tasks.
- **Recommended first round:** 3 participants; expand to 5 if possible.
- **Session length:** About 20–30 minutes.
- **Method:** Ask participants to think aloud. Do not explain how to use the interface until after the task attempt.
- **Privacy:** Use participant codes and fictional examples. Do not record customer names, private business records, or other confidential information.

## Tasks and success criteria

| Task | Prompt to read to the participant | Success criteria |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Describe a task | “Imagine you want a daily summary of new leads and a draft for your team. Show me how you would get started.” | Participant can enter or select a task and move to the plan without help. |
| 2. Understand the plan | “Tell me what you think the proposed workflow will do.” | Participant can explain the main steps and identify the data source and intended output. |
| 3. Review permissions | “Before continuing, check what the workflow can access and what needs your approval.” | Participant can identify enabled apps and explain whether a message is sent automatically. |
| 4. Inspect the preview | “Review the example output. What would you want to check before continuing?” | Participant recognises the output as an example and notices the confirmation checks. |
| 5. Complete the demo | “Continue when you are comfortable, then explain what the result screen tells you.” | Participant can complete the simulated flow and explain that no real integration or message is involved. |
| 6. Explore a problem | “Suppose one of the connected tools failed. Where would you look next?” | Participant can describe a reasonable next step or clearly identify what is missing. |

## How to summarise the results after testing

Once sessions are complete, report only what was observed:

- Number of participants and their broad task context
- Tasks completed independently, partially completed, or not completed
- Repeated misunderstandings and where they occurred
- Any high-severity issue involving permissions, approval, or mistaken expectations about external actions
- Changes made to the prototype because of the evidence
- What remains uncertain and should be tested again

Do not report a success rate, participant quote, or design improvement until it is supported by the actual sessions.
